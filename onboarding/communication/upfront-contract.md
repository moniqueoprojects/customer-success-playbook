# The Upfront Contract

One of the biggest mistakes in Customer Onboarding is jumping straight into the product.

Successful onboarding starts long before the first click.

It starts by creating alignment.

One of my favorite techniques for doing that comes from consultative selling: the **Upfront Contract**.

Although it's widely used in sales, I found that it works even better during onboarding.

Instead of starting with:

> "Let's integrate your PSA."

I prefer to establish expectations first.

---

# Why it matters

Customers naturally have questions before an implementation begins.

- How long will this take?
- What are we doing today?
- What happens next?
- What do you expect from me?

When you answer those questions before they are even asked, customers become much more relaxed.

The meeting feels organized.

The process feels predictable.

And trust starts to grow.

---

# My 50-Minute Framework

Every onboarding session follows the same structure.

## 1. Welcome

I always start by introducing myself and making the conversation feel casual.

> "Hi! It's great to meet you. Thanks so much for joining today."

Then I introduce myself.

> "I'm Mona, and I'll be helping you implement Crewhu."

Sometimes I even joke a little:

> "Honestly, you probably won't need much help because Crewhu is very easy to implement... you'll see."

Humor helps reduce anxiety.

---

## 2. Explain how onboarding works

Next, I explain the overall onboarding process.

I usually tell customers:

- Sessions last about 50 minutes.
- We can schedule as many sessions as needed.
- I like to reserve the last 5 minutes to book our next meeting.
- Once implementation is complete, we'll move into reviewing reports and measuring results.

Now they understand the journey.

---

## 3. Set today's agenda

Then I clearly explain what we are going to accomplish.

For example:

- Connect your PSA
- Configure surveys
- Test the first survey (if time allows)

Clear expectations create focused meetings.

---

## 4. Explain how we'll work together

I ask the customer to share their screen.

Instead of implementing everything myself, I guide them while they perform the configuration.

This creates confidence.

They learn the platform instead of simply watching someone else use it.

---

## 5. Ask permission before Discovery

Before asking discovery questions, I always ask for permission.

For example:

> "Before we start, I'd like to ask a few questions. Some of them may have already been discussed with our Sales team, but I'd like to confirm a few details and take some notes. Is that okay?"

It's a small detail.

But it makes the conversation collaborative instead of interrogative.

---

## 6. Encourage questions

Finally, I remind customers that the meeting is a conversation.

I usually say something like:

> "You'll probably have plenty of questions throughout the session, so please feel free to interrupt me at any time."

And my favorite line...

> "Also, let me know if I'm going too fast or too slow. I can easily bore people talking about Crewhu!" 😄

That usually gets a laugh and makes the conversation much more comfortable.

---

# Why this works

An Upfront Contract reduces uncertainty.

Instead of wondering what's happening next, customers know exactly:

- why they're here;
- what success looks like;
- how the meeting will flow;
- when they'll meet again.

The result is a smoother onboarding experience and a stronger customer relationship from day one.

---

> [!TIP]
> ## 💡 Mona's Tip
>
> **Never leave a meeting without the next one already on the calendar.**
>
> Momentum is one of the biggest predictors of onboarding success. Even if today's goals weren't fully completed, make sure everyone knows exactly what happens next and when you'll meet again. Customers feel more confident when they leave every session with a clear action plan.

---

# Final Thought

Technology doesn't build trust.

Clear communication does.

Before opening the product...

Take a few minutes to align expectations.

It may be the highest ROI conversation you'll have during onboarding.
